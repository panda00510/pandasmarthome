Digital signage sounds bigger than it is. For a small office, clinic or shop it usually means one to a handful of screens showing a menu, notices, a welcome message or the day's meetings. What you need is a screen, something to play the content, a way to change that content, and a person who will actually change it. The last part decides whether the screens stay useful.

## What a signage setup is made of

- **The screen.** A TV, a monitor, or a commercial display.
- **The player.** A small device that plays the content on the screen. It can be a separate media player box plugged into the screen, or built into a commercial display.
- **The management side.** Where you upload images and videos, arrange them, and decide which screen shows what and when.
- **The content.** Menus, promotions, notices, schedules. Something has to be made, and someone has to make it.

## Choosing the screen and the player

Start with where the screen will sit and who will look at it. A screen behind a counter, read from a metre or two away, is a different job from one in a shop window facing the street. Bright surroundings, especially direct sunlight, make an ordinary screen hard to read. Decide early whether it hangs in landscape or portrait, because layouts are designed for one or the other.

Before mounting, plan a power point behind the screen and, if possible, a network cable to it.

For the player, check which systems the signage software supports before you buy anything. ## Who updates the content

Screens that show the same thing for months stop being read. Before choosing a system, name the person who will update it, and ask how much they will have to learn.

Things that make updating realistic for a small team:

- **Using files you already have.** Panda Signage takes images and videos, plus PDF and PowerPoint files that are turned into pages automatically. Large videos get a TV-compatible copy while the original is kept.
- **Templates.** Build a screen once from blocks such as images, video, text, a clock or weather, and reuse it for the next one.
- **Small edits without the layout.** Front-desk staff can change marked text on a screen, or put an emergency notice over every screen at a location, without touching the design.
- **Roles.** Each person sees only what their role allows, so the person changing the lunch menu cannot break the lobby screen.

## What happens when the network drops

Ask this of any system: if the internet goes down at 11 am, what is on the screen at 11:05? A player that fetches every image live from the internet shows an error or a blank screen. A player that keeps a copy of its content locally keeps playing.

In Panda Signage, content is cached on each player, so a dropped connection does not blank the screen; changes reach it once it is back online. You can also export an offline pack and update a player from a USB drive. The player app itself can be updated remotely in batches, a few screens first, with the option to pause or cancel the rollout if something looks wrong.

## Self-hosted or subscription

Signage software is offered in two broad ways.

- **Subscription (cloud).** The vendor runs the server. You sign in on their website, usually pay a recurring fee, and your content sits on their servers. There is nothing to maintain, and you depend on the vendor for as long as you use it.
- **Self-hosted.** The software is installed on your own server or office network. Your content and bookings stay with you. Someone has to look after that server, as with any other system you run.

## When a meeting room door panel is worth it

A door panel shows whether a room is free and what is booked next. It earns its place when several rooms are shared, when people often walk to a room to find it taken, or when visitors need to find their way. For one meeting room that everyone can see into, a shared calendar is usually enough.

In Panda Signage, bookings are entered in the console and appear on the door panel and on room list screens. Bookings can span several days or rooms, private meetings are supported, a meeting can be shown up to four hours before it starts, and room lists can carry arrows that point people the right way. ## Common questions

### Can I use an ordinary TV?

Yes. For Panda Signage, put an Android media player running Android 11 or later behind any TV or monitor, or use a commercial display with Android built in.

### Does the screen go blank if the internet goes down?

Not with Panda Signage. Content is cached on each player and keeps playing; changes reach the screen once it is back online. A player can also be updated from a USB drive.

### Can we start with one screen?

Yes. A rollout can start with one screen and grow later.
