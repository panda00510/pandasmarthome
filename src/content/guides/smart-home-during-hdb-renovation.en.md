A smart home fits into an HDB renovation as a set of decisions, not a separate project. Your contractor and a licensed electrical worker handle the fixed wiring, your ID coordinates the trades, and your job is to say clearly what each room needs before the walls and ceilings are closed. This applies to BTO and resale flats alike.

## Who does what

- **You** decide what you want to control, where, and how much can wait.
- **Your ID or main contractor** schedules the trades, keeps the drawings and can tell you which works need approval.
- **A licensed electrical worker** does fixed electrical work: new circuits, wiring in walls, switch boxes and the distribution board.
- **The carpenter** builds curtain boxes, false ceilings and cabinets, which decide where devices can go and whether they can be reached later.
- **A smart home designer, such as us,** turns your wishes into a checklist the others can follow, and answers questions about what each device needs. We do not replace your ID or electrician.

## HDB rules: check, do not assume

HDB publishes renovation guidelines for flats, and some works need an HDB permit before they start. HDB also keeps a Directory of Renovation Contractors. The rules can change and they depend on the work, so read HDB's current guidelines and ask your contractor what applies to your flat.

Most smart home preparation is ordinary electrical and carpentry work: extra wiring, switch positions, network cables and power points. It still has to follow the same rules as any other renovation work in the flat.

## What to do at each stage

### Before the contractor is confirmed

Ask each contractor you are considering how they handle extra wiring, network cables and changes to switch positions, and who their electrical worker is. A clear answer here saves arguments later. If you have just collected the keys of a BTO flat, our [key collection guide](/guides/bto-key-collection-smart-home-timeline/) covers the order of decisions.

### Layout and design

Agree furniture, bed and TV positions first. Then give your ID one written list per room: where you want control, where you need power and where you need a network cable. Our [renovation checklist](/guides/renovation-smart-home-prep/) lists what to include.

### Hacking and wiring

This is when changes are cheapest.

- **Neutral wires** at the switch boxes you may automate. If some boxes will not have one, see [no-neutral smart switches](/guides/no-neutral-smart-switches/).
- **LAN cables** to the rooms that need them. See our [Wi-Fi guide](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/).
- **Power points** near curtain boxes, the main door and where a hub or access point will sit.
- **Distribution board.** If you plan circuit-level energy monitoring, ask your electrical worker whether there is space in the board.

In a resale flat, ask the electrical worker to check the existing wiring before new work is added. Our [HDB resale page](/homes/hdb-resale-smart-home/) and [older home checklist](/guides/check-older-home-before-smart/) explain what to look for.

### Carpentry and false ceilings

- Confirm curtain box depth for any motorised track you are considering.
- Leave access panels for anything above the ceiling.
- Check that an aircon controller will still be able to see each indoor unit once cabinets are in.

### Before handover

Walk through the flat with your list. Check that every switch box, cable and power point is where you asked. Label the LAN cables at both ends if they are not already labelled.

### After move-in

Devices, sensors, scenes and automations. Most of them are wireless and can be added room by room.

## Common questions

### Do I need an HDB permit for smart home work?

It depends on the work. Some renovation works need an HDB permit and some do not. Check HDB's current guidelines and ask your contractor which of your planned works need one and who will apply for it.

### Can my smart home installer do the electrical wiring?

Fixed electrical work should be done by a licensed electrical worker. Plug-in and battery devices do not need wiring work at all. We plan with your contractor's electrical worker rather than bringing a separate crew.

### My renovation is almost finished. Is it too late?

No. Retrofit devices, no-neutral switches and battery sensors work in most finished flats. What becomes harder is moving switch positions or adding cables inside closed walls and ceilings.

### Should I choose devices before the renovation starts?

Choose the approach, not every product. Knowing whether you want motorised curtains or a smart lock tells the contractor what to prepare. The exact models can wait until the layout is final.

## A simple way to start

Write one page per room with three lines: control, power, network. Give it to your ID before the wiring quote and check it again before the ceilings close. You can [print our one-page checklist](/renovation-checklist/) and fill it in.
