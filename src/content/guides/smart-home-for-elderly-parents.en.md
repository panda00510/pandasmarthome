When a parent is getting older, most families want two things at once: to know they are safe, and to let them live in their own home without feeling watched. Technology can help with the first without ruining the second, if you pick the right tools and talk to the person first.

## Start with the person, not the gadgets

Before choosing anything, ask what actually worries you and what your parent is comfortable with.

1. **What is the real risk?** A fall in the bathroom at night, a door left open, a stove or tap left on, or simply not knowing whether they are up and about.
2. **Who will receive an alert, and who will act on it?** An alert nobody answers is worse than none.
3. **Does your parent agree?** A system they have chosen and understand is used. One installed behind their back is switched off or resented.

## Things that help without any camera

- **Night lighting.** Soft lights that come on with movement between the bed and the bathroom are one of the most useful changes for lowering the chance of a trip in the dark.
- **A door sensor** on the front door or the bedroom shows whether the door has been opened late at night, or has not opened at all by the usual time in the morning.
- **A motion or presence sensor** shows that there is normal activity, such as someone moving around in the morning, without showing what they are doing.
- **Leak, smoke and gas sensors** that alert you and the household. See [energy monitoring and safety sensors](/solutions/energy-monitoring/).
- **A wearable or wall button** that your parent can press to call for help. Its strength is that your parent decides when to use it.

## Keep the controls simple

A system that needs an app will not help someone who does not use one. Keep the basics physical and obvious.

- **Wall switches that keep working** exactly as before.
- **A few clearly labelled buttons,** such as one for bedtime or one for leaving the house.
- **No required login** for your parent. See [controls for the whole household](/solutions/home-control/).

## Doors, helpers and visitors

If a helper or a family member needs to get in, a keyless lock with individual codes avoids sharing one key. See [smart door locks](/guides/smart-door-lock-guide/) for the backup plans to check first.

## Respect and privacy

- **Tell them what is installed and what it can see.** A motion sensor knows someone is moving. It does not know what they are doing.
- **Agree who gets alerts** and what counts as worth an alert.
- **Keep the data at home where you can.** A system that runs on a controller in the house does not have to send daily routines to a company. See [what still works without internet](/guides/smart-home-without-internet/).
- **Review it together** after a few weeks. Remove what feels intrusive.

## What technology cannot do

A sensor can tell you that something looks unusual. It cannot replace a visit, a phone call or proper care. It can also raise false alarms, so plan how your family will check in when an alert arrives. If your parent has medical needs, speak to their doctor or care provider about the right alert service.

## Common questions

### Do I need cameras to look after a parent at home?

No. Night lighting, door and motion sensors, safety sensors and a call button give a lot of reassurance without any camera.

### Will my parent feel watched?

It depends on how you introduce it. Involve them, keep to sensors that do not record images or sound, and agree what you will and will not look at.

### Can this work if my parent has a domestic helper?

Yes. Sensors and night lights work with whoever is in the home, and a keyless lock can give the helper their own code. Tell the helper what is installed.

### Is a smart home a substitute for a medical alert service?

No. It is a layer of reassurance, not a medical device. For health needs, ask the doctor or care provider about proper alert services.

## A simple way to start

Begin with night lighting between the bed and the bathroom, plus one door sensor. Live with it for a few weeks, then add only what your parent is happy to have.
