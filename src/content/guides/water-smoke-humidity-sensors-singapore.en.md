Singapore homes deal with water, heat and humidity every day. Most problems start small: a slow drip under the sink, a damp corner in a wardrobe, a pot left on the stove. Simple sensors can tell you early, which is when the problem is still easy to fix.

## Water leak sensors

A leak sensor sits on the floor and raises an alert when it gets wet. It is the simplest safety device to add and one of the most useful.

- **Good places:** under the kitchen sink, behind the washing machine, near the water heater, under the aircon drain and by the toilet cistern.
- **What it can do:** warn you or the household, and in some setups trigger a siren so that someone in the home hears it.
- **What it cannot do:** it only detects water where it is placed. It cannot stop a leak by itself unless you also fit an automatic shut-off valve, which is plumbing work.

## Smoke and gas

Smoke detection is a fire safety matter, and it is different from the other sensors here. The Singapore Civil Defence Force (SCDF) describes a home fire alarm device (HFAD) as an automatic smoke or heat detector that sounds an alarm to alert the people at home, and recommends one for the primary protection of homes. On its page, installation is mandatory in new homes and in existing homes that carry out certain fire safety works, and strongly encouraged in other existing homes. Read the current details, including which homes are covered, on the [SCDF Home Fire Alarm Device (HFAD) page](https://www.scdf.gov.sg/fire-safety-services-listing/home-fire-alarm-device-hfad), and contact SCDF if you are unsure about your home.

- **A smart or connected detector** can send an alert to your phone and to other people when you are away. SCDF's page does not cover connected detectors, so do not treat one as a replacement for a home fire alarm device. Use it to add alerts.
- **A gas sensor** is useful where a gas stove or heater is in use. Check which type suits your home and follow the maker's instructions for placement.

## Humidity and temperature sensors

In a humid climate these help you act before mould appears.

- **Where to place them:** wardrobes, storerooms, bedrooms with little airflow and near a window that often stays closed.
- **What to do with the numbers:** use them to decide when to run a dehumidifier or a fan, or to check whether the aircon is leaving a room damp.
- **Automations:** a sensor can switch a dehumidifier on and off at the levels you set, so you do not have to remember.

## How alerts should work

An alert is only useful if someone acts on it.

1. **Decide who gets it,** and who is the back-up when that person is away.
2. **Keep a local siren** for serious cases, so someone in the home hears even when the phone is not nearby.
3. **Know what happens when the internet is down.** Detection and automations that run on a controller in your home keep working. Notifications to a phone need a connection. See [what still works without internet](/guides/smart-home-without-internet/).

## Common questions

### Do water leak sensors work without Wi-Fi?

Some use Zigbee or another low-power link to a controller instead of Wi-Fi, and some are Wi-Fi only. A local siren works either way. Choose according to how you want alerts delivered.

### Can a sensor shut off the water automatically?

Only with a shut-off valve, which is plumbing work. A sensor on its own raises the alert.

### Do I still need a normal smoke detector?

Yes. Follow SCDF's guidance for home fire alarm devices. A connected sensor can add alerts to your phone, but it should not be your only protection.

### How often should I check the batteries?

Follow the maker's instructions for testing and replacing them. Many sensors tell you when the battery is low.

## A simple way to start

Put a leak sensor under the kitchen sink and one by the washing machine, then a humidity sensor in the wardrobe that worries you most. Add more only where you have a real concern.
