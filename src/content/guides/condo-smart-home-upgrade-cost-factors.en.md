The cost of a condo smart home upgrade comes mostly from scope and from the state of what is already in the walls, not from the brand of switch. How many rooms, which devices, whether walls need to be opened, how curtains, locks and aircon connect, how good the network is and what your MCST allows all shape the quote. To compare quotes fairly, give every installer the same room-by-room list.

We do not publish package prices, because a number without these details is not meaningful. This guide explains what goes into a quote so that you can read one.

## Scope: rooms and what you want to control

The biggest factor is how much you ask for. Lighting in the living room is a different job from lighting, curtains, aircon and sensors in every room. Write down, room by room, what you want to control. Leaving rooms for later is a normal way to keep the first phase small.

## Device choice

- **Smart switches or smart bulbs.** They solve different problems. See [smart switch or smart bulb](/guides/smart-switch-or-smart-bulb/).
- **Wired or battery devices.** Battery sensors need no wiring work but need battery changes. Wired devices need an electrical worker.
- **Protocol and platform.** Zigbee, Thread, Matter and Wi-Fi devices differ in what they need around them. See [what Matter is](/guides/what-is-matter/).

## The state of the wiring

This is often where quotes differ most.

- **Neutral wires.** If switch boxes have a neutral, more switch options are available. If not, there are [no-neutral smart switches](/guides/no-neutral-smart-switches/), with trade-offs.
- **Back box depth.** Some smart switches need more space than an ordinary switch.
- **Opening walls or not.** Running new cables in a finished condo means chasing walls or ceilings, then patching and repainting. A wireless-first plan avoids most of that.
- **The distribution board.** Circuit-level energy monitoring needs space in the board and a licensed electrical worker.

An installer who has not seen your switch boxes is quoting on assumptions. Ask whether a site check is included before the final quote.

## Curtains, locks and aircon

- **Curtains.** Track or roller, wired or battery motor, and whether the existing curtain box is deep enough. See the [motorised curtains guide](/guides/motorised-curtains-guide/).
- **Door locks.** Whether the door and frame suit the lock you want, and any rules your MCST has about the main door. See the [smart door lock guide](/guides/smart-door-lock-guide/).
- **Aircon.** Infrared control is simple. A wired adapter depends on the brand and model. See [smart aircon control](/guides/smart-aircon-control/).

## Network

Large condo units and thick walls can leave rooms with weak Wi-Fi. If the network needs access points or new cables, that belongs in the quote too. See our [Wi-Fi guide](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/).

## Hub and platform

A local hub or controller in your home lets automations run without the internet. Cloud apps from device makers can be simpler to start with. The choice affects both the hardware in the quote and the setup work. See [local smart home vs Google, Apple and SmartThings](/guides/local-smart-home-vs-google-apple-smartthings/).

## Labour, coordination and MCST rules

- **Installation and setup.** Fitting devices is one part. Pairing, naming, scenes and automations take time too.
- **Coordination.** If an ID, electrical worker or curtain supplier is also involved, someone has to coordinate them.
- **MCST rules.** Renovation approval, by-laws and permitted working hours vary between developments. Some works may need approval, deposits or particular hours. Check with your management office before work is scheduled, because it affects how the job can be planned. See our [condo smart home page](/homes/condo-smart-home/).

## How to get a comparable quote

1. **Write one list per room** of what you want to control.
2. **Send the same list to every installer,** with photos of the switch boxes, curtain areas, aircon units and main door.
3. **Ask for a site check** before the final quote, so the wiring state is seen, not guessed.
4. **Ask for labour, devices and coordination to be listed separately.**
5. **Ask what happens without internet** and who maintains the system afterwards.
6. **Share your MCST's renovation rules** so the plan fits them from the start.

## Common questions

### Why do two quotes for the same condo look so different?

Usually because they cover different scope or assume different wiring. One may include network work and setup, the other not. Asking both to quote the same room-by-room list makes the difference visible.

### Can I keep the cost down without opening walls?

Often, yes. Battery sensors, no-neutral switches, smart plugs and infrared aircon control avoid most wiring work. The trade-off is usually in choice of devices or battery changes.

### Do I need MCST approval for a smart home upgrade?

It depends on the work and your development. Fixed wiring, door changes and anything touching the facade may be covered by your MCST's rules. Check with your management office before work begins.

### Can I upgrade one room first and add more later?

Yes. Start with the rooms you use most, on a platform that can grow. Planning the network and hub for the whole unit at the start makes later rooms simpler.

## A simple way to start

List each room, what you want to control there and a photo of the switch box. That one document is the basis of any comparable quote.
