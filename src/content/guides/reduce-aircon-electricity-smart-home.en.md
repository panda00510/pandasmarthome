In a Singapore home the aircon is usually the biggest single use of electricity, and it is also the hardest to judge by feel. You see one number at the end of the month, but not which unit ran, when, or why. Measuring first and then automating is a calmer way to cut waste than guessing.

## Step 1: find out what is actually running

You cannot change what you cannot see. There are three levels of measurement, from simple to thorough.

- **A smart plug with power measurement** shows what one plug-in appliance uses. It suits a fridge, a dehumidifier or a fan, but not a ceiling-mounted aircon, which is wired in.
- **A power meter on a circuit** shows what a particular circuit draws, such as one aircon. Installing it inside the distribution board is electrical work for a licensed electrician.
- **A whole-home meter** shows the total through the day. Seeing the total beside the aircon lets you tell how much of the load is cooling.

Start with the question you want answered. Often a few days of data on the aircon circuits is enough.

## Step 2: look for the common patterns

When people see their numbers for the first time, the same things tend to appear.

1. **An aircon that runs in an empty room**, usually because someone forgot or left a window open.
2. **A unit that runs all night at the same setting** when the room has already cooled.
3. **Standby loads** from TVs, set-top boxes and chargers that add up while no one is using them.
4. **A single appliance** that uses far more than you expected.

## Step 3: add automations that match how you live

Automations only help if they fit the household. Some that are worth considering:

- **Switch off when the room is empty or a window is open,** using a presence or door and window sensor.
- **Ease the setting once you are asleep,** or switch off at a set time.
- **Cool the room shortly before you arrive,** not hours earlier.
- **Remind you** when the aircon has been on for a long time with no one in the room.

We do not promise a particular saving. What you save depends on the home, the unit and the habits, which is why measuring first matters. See [smart aircon control](/guides/smart-aircon-control/) for how the units connect.

## Check the energy label when you buy

Singapore's National Environment Agency runs a mandatory energy labelling scheme for several household appliances, including air-conditioners, and you will see a tick rating on the label. The ratings have been tightened over time, so compare units on the current label rather than from memory. The tick rating describes efficiency under test conditions. It is a useful way to compare models, but it does not tell you what your own bill will be. Check the NEA website for the current rules.

## Common questions

### How do I know which aircon uses the most electricity?

Measure each unit's circuit, or use a power-measuring device where the aircon plugs in. Without measurement it is hard to tell, because the same unit can use very different amounts depending on how it is used.

### Do I need an electrician to monitor power?

For plug-in appliances, no. A smart plug is enough. For a circuit or the whole home, the meter goes in the distribution board, which is work for a licensed electrician.

### Will a smart aircon controller lower my bill?

It can help where waste comes from forgetting or leaving a window open. It does not change how much a unit uses when it is running. The result depends on your habits.

### Does the tick rating predict my bill?

Not directly. It compares efficiency under test conditions, which helps when choosing between models, but your bill also depends on room size, how often you use it and the setting.

## A simple way to start

Pick the room where the aircon runs most and measure it for a week. Then decide what, if anything, you want to automate.
