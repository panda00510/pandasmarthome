A smart home learns when you wake up, when you leave, which rooms you use and who is at the door. That information is useful to you. Whether it also ends up on someone else's servers depends on how the system is built.

## What a smart home can know

- **Routines:** when lights go on and off, when doors open and when rooms are empty.
- **Presence:** whether anyone is home, and which room they are in.
- **Energy use:** which appliances run and when, which hints at daily habits.
- **Images and sound:** from cameras, doorbells and voice assistants, if you have them.

None of this is a problem if it stays under your control. The question is where it is stored and who can see it.

## Local control and cloud control

- **Cloud control** sends your commands and often your sensor data to the company's servers. It is easy to set up, and it depends on the internet and on the company's service and policies.
- **Local control** processes things on a controller in your home. Automations and dashboards run on your own network, and less data needs to leave the house.

Most real systems mix the two. The practical question is which data leaves your home, and whether you can choose. See [what still works without internet](/guides/smart-home-without-internet/) and [how to choose a platform](/guides/local-smart-home-vs-google-apple-smartthings/).

## Questions to ask about any device

1. **Does it need an account** with the maker, and can it work without one?
2. **Where is the data stored,** in your home or on the company's servers?
3. **Does it record images or sound,** and where do the recordings go?
4. **What happens if the company changes its terms** or stops supporting the product?
5. **Can you delete your data** and remove the device cleanly?

## Cameras need extra thought

A camera sees people, and that raises more questions than a sensor. Where possible, use sensors that do not record images or sound. If you do use cameras, tell the people who live in or visit the home, including a domestic helper. ## What the law says

This section is general information and is not legal advice.

Singapore's Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) publishes guidance on the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA). In general, the PDPA's data protection rules apply to organisations, and an individual acting in a personal or domestic capacity, for example someone using a device at home for family purposes, is not covered by them. Businesses and other organisations that handle personal data can have obligations, and a device that records people outside your own home may raise other questions. For your own situation, check the current guidance on the [PDPC website](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg) or get professional advice.

## Practical steps

- **Keep devices on your own Wi-Fi network,** with a strong password, and update them.
- **Use a separate network for smart devices** if your router allows it. See [Wi-Fi for a smart home](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/).
- **Turn off features you do not use,** such as voice recording history.
- **Use unique passwords** for every account tied to a device.
- **Choose devices that can be controlled locally** where you can.
- **Review what is connected** every few months and remove what you no longer use.

## Common questions

### Is local control always more private?

It usually keeps more data at home, but it depends on the devices and settings. A local system can still connect to cloud services, so check what each device does.

### Do smart speakers listen all the time?

They listen for a wake word, and what happens next depends on the product and its settings. Read the maker's privacy information and review the options in the app.

### Can someone hack my smart home?

Any connected device can be a target. Strong passwords, updates, a separate network for smart devices and fewer unused devices reduce the risk.

### Is local control harder to set up?

It can take more setup effort, and a good installer can handle that. Ask any supplier what ongoing costs, if any, apply to each option.

## A simple way to start

List every connected device you have and note which ones need the cloud. Replace or limit the ones that collect more than they need to.
