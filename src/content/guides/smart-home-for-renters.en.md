Renting a flat or condo unit does not mean giving up on a smart home. It does change the rules of the game: anything you install has to come out again when you leave, and anything that touches the building needs the owner's agreement first. The good news is that a lot of useful automation needs neither.

## Start with the tenancy agreement

Before buying anything, read what your tenancy agreement says about alterations, drilling and fixtures. Many agreements ask tenants to hand the unit back in its original condition. If something on your list involves replacing a fixture, get the landlord's consent **in writing** — a short message that names the item is far better than a verbal "should be fine".

## What works without any alterations

- **Smart bulbs** in the existing light fittings. Leave the wall switch on and control the lights from an app, a schedule or a wireless button.
- **Smart plugs** for lamps, fans and other appliances with a simple on/off switch. Check the plug's rated load before using it on anything that draws a lot of power.
- **Battery-powered sensors** for doors, windows, motion, temperature and water leaks. They can sit on a shelf or be attached with removable adhesive.
- **Wireless scene buttons** mounted with adhesive next to the door or the bed.
- **Infrared (IR) controllers** that learn your air-conditioner and TV remotes, so the aircon can be scheduled or switched off when everyone leaves.
- **Curtain robots** that clip onto an existing rail or rod and push the curtain along, instead of a motor that replaces the track.

## What usually needs the landlord's consent

- **Replacing wall switches.** It is also fixed-wiring work, which in Singapore should be carried out by a licensed electrical worker.
- **Replacing the main door lock.** Agree with the landlord on who keeps the original lock and how access is handed back at the end of the tenancy.
- **Motorised curtain tracks, ceiling-mounted devices, or anything drilled into walls and ceilings.**
- **Cameras facing outside your unit**, such as the corridor. Check with the landlord and any building management rules first, and think about your neighbours.

## Keep it portable

1. **Choose a hub or controller you own**, not one tied to the unit. It moves with you and keeps all your automations.
2. **Favour open standards** such as Zigbee, Thread or Matter, so devices from different brands keep working together in the next home.
3. **Keep the original parts** — bulbs, switch plates, locks, curtain hooks — in a labelled box. Moving out is much easier when you can put everything back as it was.
4. **Note where each device is**, so nothing stays hidden behind the sofa when you leave.

## When you move out

Remove each device from your app and hub, then factory-reset it, before you pack it or leave it for the next occupant. A device still linked to your account can keep reporting to you, or let the next person see it in theirs. If you added anything the landlord agreed to keep, hand over the manuals and make sure it is no longer tied to your account.

## Wi-Fi and the router

If the router came with the unit, you may not be able to change its settings. Many smart home devices work best when they do not depend on Wi-Fi at all — a Zigbee or Thread network run from your own hub avoids crowding a shared router with dozens of gadgets.
