Most smart switches need constant power for their radio, and that normally comes from a neutral wire. In many homes, though, the switch box only holds the live wire and the wire running to the light — the neutral goes straight to the light fitting instead. Older HDB flats are often wired this way, so it is worth checking before you buy switches.

## How to tell whether you have a neutral

In Singapore, work on fixed wiring should be carried out by a licensed electrical worker, and that includes opening a switch box. They will look for a neutral conductor next to the live and switched wires — blue in newer wiring, black in older wiring.

## How no-neutral switches work

A no-neutral switch powers itself by passing a very small current through the light, even when the light is off. Old incandescent bulbs never noticed it. Low-wattage LEDs can: the result is a light that **flickers or glows faintly when it should be off**.

Common fixes:

- A bypass capacitor fitted at the light (many switches ship with one)
- A higher total LED load on that circuit
- LED lights or drivers known to work with no-neutral switches

## Trade-offs

- **Mesh strength:** Zigbee no-neutral switches usually act as end devices rather than repeaters, so they do not strengthen the mesh the way mains-powered switches with a neutral do.
- **Minimum load:** many models state a minimum wattage per gang — check it against your lights.
- **Choice:** there are fewer no-neutral models to pick from.

## Alternatives

1. Run a neutral to the switch box. The cleanest result, and easiest during a renovation.
2. Fit a relay module at the light fitting, where the neutral already is, and keep your existing switch.
3. Use smart bulbs and leave the wall switch on, with a wireless scene button beside it.
