A smart home is rarely used by one person. There are family members, a domestic helper, children, grandparents and visitors, and each needs different things. The most common failure is a system that only the person who set it up knows how to use. Deciding who gets access to what, before you buy, avoids that.

## Start with the people

List who is in the home and what each person actually needs to do.

1. **The household admin** changes settings and fixes things.
2. **Adults in the family** use scenes, lights and aircon, and may need door access.
3. **A domestic helper** lives and works in the home, and needs to get in and out and to turn lights and aircon on and off. This should not require a personal account.
4. **Children** need simple controls and safe limits.
5. **Guests and visitors** need to get in sometimes and to use lights and the aircon without an app.

## Physical controls first

Anyone can use a wall switch or a keypad without installing anything.

- **Keep the wall switches working** exactly as before.
- **Add a few scene buttons** by the door and the bed, such as one for leaving the house.
- **Label them clearly** in the language people read. See [controls for the whole household](/solutions/home-control/).

## Doors

A keyless lock with individual codes gives each person their own way in, and lets you remove one person's access without changing keys for everyone.

- **A code for each person** works better than one shared code.
- **Temporary codes** suit a visitor or a contractor and can be removed afterwards.
- **A backup way in** matters, in case of a flat battery or a fault. See [smart door locks](/guides/smart-door-lock-guide/) for the backup plans to check.
- **Entry logs:** if the lock keeps a log, tell the household that it exists and what it is for. Use it for practical things, such as working out when a code was last used, and not to watch people.

## Children

- **Keep the controls simple.** Large buttons or a wall panel work better than an app on a phone they may not have.
- **Limit what can go wrong.** For example, decide whether children can change the aircon temperature or unlock the door.
- **Think about the door.** Decide whether a child can open the main door with a code and what should happen if they come home early.

## A domestic helper

A helper works in your home and also lives in it. Set the system up so that it makes their day easier, and is open with them.

- **Explain it before it goes live.** Show how the switches, scene buttons and door code work, in the language they are most comfortable in, and invite their questions.
- **Agree what is expected,** of the system and of everyone using it, and ask what would make their work easier.
- **Give door access with an individual code** rather than lending a key. Remove it only when it is no longer needed, for example when the job ends.
- **Be open about every device.** Tell the helper what is installed, what it senses and whether anything is recorded.
- **Keep their own space private.** No camera or recording device in a helper's room, and no use of sensors or entry logs to track their time off.
- **Do not require a personal phone account** or an app on their own phone.

## Visitors

Visitors should be able to turn on a light and the aircon without help.

- **Wall switches and keypads** cover this.
- **A guest mode** can set sensible defaults, for example an aircon that switches off after a few hours.

## Privacy and fairness

- **Say what is recorded.** If a doorbell or lock keeps a log of entries, tell the people it affects.
- **Avoid cameras inside the home** where people live. Use sensors that do not record images or sound where you can. See [smart home privacy](/guides/smart-home-privacy-local-vs-cloud/).
- **Review the rules with the household** once the system has been in use for a few weeks.

## Common questions

### Does my helper need to install an app?

No. Physical switches, wall keypads and a door code cover most daily tasks without an app or account.

### Will the system track my helper?

It should not. Choose devices that do not record people, tell the helper what is installed, and keep entry logs for practical use only.

### Can I give a visitor a temporary door code?

On most keyless locks, yes. Check that your lock supports temporary codes and how long they last.

### Should children be able to change the settings?

Let them use simple controls such as lights, and keep settings and the door under adult control. Decide the limits together as a household.

### What if the helper or a child loses their code?

Remove it and issue a new one. This is a good reason to use an individual code for each person instead of a shared one.

## A simple way to start

Write down who needs to do what in each room, and set up physical controls and door codes for those needs first.
