Smart devices do not all talk the same way. The wireless link a device uses decides how long its battery lasts, how many you can add and what you need in the home to run it. Knowing the difference helps you buy fewer wrong things.

## Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi is the link your phone and laptop already use.

- **Good for:** cameras, speakers, TV boxes and anything that sends a lot of data.
- **Watch out for:** every device is another client on your router. Dozens of small Wi-Fi gadgets can crowd a router that was fine for phones.
- **Battery devices** on Wi-Fi tend to drain faster, so makers often pick another link for those.

## Zigbee

Zigbee is a low-power mesh link made for small devices.

- **Good for:** door sensors, temperature sensors, buttons, smart switches and bulbs.
- **How it works:** devices that are plugged into the mains can pass messages along, so a larger home gets better coverage as you add them. Battery devices usually do not relay.
- **What you need:** a gateway or controller that speaks Zigbee. It does not run on a normal router.
- **Watch out for:** Zigbee shares the 2.4 GHz band with Wi-Fi. Choose its channel so it does not sit on top of your busiest Wi-Fi channel.

## Thread

Thread is also a low-power mesh, and it is the link many newer Matter devices use. See [what is Matter](/guides/what-is-matter/).

- **Good for:** battery sensors, locks and other small devices that are Matter-ready.
- **What you need:** a Thread border router. Some speakers, TV boxes and gateways include one. Check before you buy Thread devices.

## Bluetooth

Bluetooth is common in locks and small gadgets for setup and close range.

- **Good for:** the first connection, and short-range control.
- **Watch out for:** range is short, so a lock that only uses Bluetooth may not reach you when you are away. See [smart door locks](/guides/smart-door-lock-guide/).

## Other links you may see

Some devices use another sub-GHz link or a maker's own radio. Check what the device needs before you buy, and whether it can work with the controller you plan to use.

## How to choose

1. **Start with the controller.** Decide what will run the home, then buy devices it can talk to.
2. **Put the controller centrally,** not in a cupboard in a far corner.
3. **Use mains-powered Zigbee or Thread devices** to build the mesh before you add battery ones.
4. **Keep Wi-Fi for the heavy devices,** and see [Wi-Fi for a smart home](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/).
5. **Keep a few wired points** where you can, such as a network cable to the controller.

## Common questions

### Do I need all of them?

No. Many homes need one low-power link for sensors and switches, plus Wi-Fi for the rest.

### Will Zigbee slow down my Wi-Fi?

It can interfere if the channels overlap. Choosing a channel away from your main Wi-Fi channel usually avoids the problem.

### Does Matter replace Zigbee?

Matter describes how devices talk, and it can run over Wi-Fi or Thread. Existing Zigbee devices can still be useful, and some controllers can bridge them.

### Can a renter use these?

Yes. Most are battery or plug-in devices. See [smart home when you rent](/guides/smart-home-for-renters/).

## A simple way to start

Write down the devices you want in each room and mark which ones are small battery devices and which send a lot of data. That tells you which link you need.
