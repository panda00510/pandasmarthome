After BTO key collection, the order matters more than the shopping list. Survey the flat, agree the layout with your interior designer or contractor, then settle wiring, neutral wires and network cables before carpentry and any false ceiling go up. Devices come last, and many of them can wait until after you move in.

The steps below follow the way a typical BTO renovation unfolds. Your ID or contractor sets the actual schedule, so treat this as a sequence of decisions, not a timetable.

## 1. Survey the flat as it was handed over

Before anyone draws a plan, walk through the empty flat and note what is already there.

- **Switch and socket positions** as built, and which lights each switch controls.
- **The distribution board,** where it is and roughly how many circuits it has.
- **Where the network and telecom points are.** In many flats they sit near the service area or a corner of the living room.
- **Windows and aircon positions,** including where indoor units are planned to go.

This survey is also the moment to open a switch box and check whether a neutral wire is present, ideally with your electrician. Our [no-neutral smart switches guide](/guides/no-neutral-smart-switches/) explains why it matters.

## 2. Fix the layout with your ID or contractor

The layout decides where people walk, sit and sleep, so it decides where switches, sensors and access points should go. Settle furniture positions, bed positions and the TV wall before asking for a wiring quote, or you will be moving points twice.

Tell your ID early that you want a smart home. They do not need to know every device, only what each room needs: control points, power and network. Our [BTO planning page](/homes/bto-smart-home/) describes how we prepare that checklist alongside your ID and electrician.

## 3. Wiring, neutral wires and LAN runs before carpentry

This is the stage that is hard to undo. Once carpentry, feature walls and false ceilings are in, changing a cable means opening finished work.

- **Neutral wires** at the switch boxes you may want to automate. This gives you a wider choice of smart switches later.
- **Switch positions** that match how you will use each room, such as both sides of the bed and the entrance.
- **LAN cables** to the living room, study and any spot where an access point, hub or TV will sit. See our [Wi-Fi guide](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/) for placement.
- **Power points** for things that will need them: near curtain boxes if you plan wired motors, near the door if a smart lock needs it, and where a hub or access point will live.

Wiring in walls and switch boxes should be done by a licensed electrical worker. HDB has its own renovation guidelines, so check them and ask your contractor what applies. Our guide on [smart home work during an HDB renovation](/guides/smart-home-during-hdb-renovation/) covers who does what at each stage.

## 4. Curtain box depth and false ceilings

Curtain boxes and false ceilings are built by carpenters, often before anyone has chosen a curtain motor.

- **Curtain box depth.** A motorised track needs room for the motor and the track. Give your carpenter the space needed for the system you are considering. Our [motorised curtains guide](/guides/motorised-curtains-guide/) explains the options.
- **Access panels.** Leave a way to reach anything placed above a false ceiling, such as an aircon adapter, a power supply or a junction point.
- **Aircon line of sight.** If you plan infrared control, think about whether a controller will be able to see each indoor unit once cabinets are built.

## 5. Devices last

Once the walls are closed and the layout is final, choose devices. Products change; wiring does not. Buying everything before renovation often means returning items that do not fit the final switch boxes or curtain boxes.

## What can wait until after move-in

- **Scenes and automations.** Live in the flat first. Your routines will tell you what is worth automating.
- **Sensors.** Most leak, door and humidity sensors are battery powered and stick in place. See [leak, smoke and humidity sensors](/guides/water-smoke-humidity-sensors-singapore/).
- **Smart plugs and IR controllers** for aircon and fans.
- **Additional rooms.** Start with the rooms you use most, then extend.

## Common questions

### Is it too late if my ID has already started?

Not necessarily. If carpentry and false ceilings are not yet built, wiring and network points can often still be adjusted. If they are, retrofit devices and no-neutral switches still work in most flats.

### Do I need to buy smart switches before renovation?

No. What you need before renovation is the wiring: neutral wires, back boxes and switch positions. The switches themselves can be chosen once the layout is final.

### Who should I tell first, my ID or my electrician?

Usually your ID or main contractor, because they coordinate the trades. Give them a written list of what each room needs, so the electrician and carpenter work from the same page.

### What if I am buying a resale flat instead?

The order is similar, but start with a check of the existing wiring. See our [HDB resale page](/homes/hdb-resale-smart-home/) and [what to check in an older home](/guides/check-older-home-before-smart/).

## A simple way to start

Take your floor plan and mark three things in each room: where you want control, where you need power and where you need a network cable. Hand that page to your ID before the wiring quote.
