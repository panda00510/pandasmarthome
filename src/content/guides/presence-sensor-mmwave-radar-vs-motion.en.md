"Turn the light off when nobody is in the room" sounds simple, until the light goes off while you are reading on the sofa. That is the difference between a motion sensor and a presence sensor. A motion sensor sees movement; a presence sensor tries to tell whether someone is still there. Choosing the right one, and putting it in the right place, decides whether an automation feels helpful or annoying.

## Motion sensors (PIR)

A passive infrared (PIR) sensor notices when something warm moves across its view. It is the small sensor most people picture.

- **Good at:** reacting quickly when someone walks in, running for a long time on a battery, and costing little.
- **Not good at:** noticing a person who is sitting or lying still. After a set time with no movement, it reports the room as empty.
- **Best for:** corridors, entrances, store rooms and toilets where people walk in, do something and leave.

## mmWave radar presence sensors

A millimetre-wave (mmWave) radar sensor sends out a low-power radio signal and measures what comes back. It can pick up very small movements, such as breathing or typing, so it can tell that someone is still in the room.

- **Good at:** keeping a room "occupied" while someone reads, works or watches TV. Many models can also report roughly where in the room the person is, or ignore chosen zones.
- **Not good at:** ignoring things that move by themselves. A ceiling fan, a swinging curtain or a pet can look like a person. Radar can also sense through thin walls, doors and glass, so it may react to the room next door.
- **Power:** most radar sensors run from mains or USB power rather than a battery, so plan a power point.
- **Best for:** living rooms, studies, bedrooms and bathrooms where people stay still.

Radar presence sensing records no images and no sound. Our guide to [fall detection with radar](/guides/radar-fall-detection/) covers a related use and its limits.

## Use both in the same room

A common setup pairs the two. The motion sensor turns the light on the moment someone walks in; the radar sensor keeps it on while anyone is still there; the room is only treated as empty when both agree. This avoids the short delay some radar sensors have before they report, and the light that switches off on a still person.

## Where to put them

- **Keep fans and aircon flaps out of a radar sensor's view,** or exclude that zone in its settings. In Singapore homes with ceiling fans, this is the most common cause of a room that never reads as empty.
- **Avoid aiming at curtains and windows,** which move in the wind or with the aircon.
- **Mind the wall behind it.** If the next room is on the other side of a thin wall, turn down the range or move the sensor.
- **Do not point a motion sensor at heat sources or direct sun,** which can trigger it.
- **Cover where people sit,** not just the door. For a study, aim at the desk; for a living room, at the sofa.

After installing, walk through the room, then sit still for a few minutes, and check how the sensor reports both.

## What presence sensing is used for

- **Lights** that come on when you walk in and stay on while you are there.
- **Aircon** that switches off when a room has been empty for a while. See [where the electricity goes in a Singapore home](/guides/reduce-aircon-electricity-smart-home/).
- **Bathroom lights and exhaust fans** that stay on during a shower.
- **A gentle check on an older family member,** such as normal activity in the morning. See [smart home for elderly parents](/guides/smart-home-for-elderly-parents/).

## Which connection to choose

Presence and motion sensors come in Zigbee, Thread or Matter, and Wi-Fi versions. Battery motion sensors are usually Zigbee or Thread, which use little power. Mains-powered radar sensors are often Wi-Fi or Zigbee. Pick one your controller supports locally, so lights still respond when the internet is down; see [what still works without internet](/guides/smart-home-without-internet/).

## Common questions

### Will the lights turn off while I sit still?

With only a motion sensor, they can, once the timer runs out. A radar presence sensor, or a motion and radar pair, keeps the room occupied while you are there.

### Can a radar sensor see through walls?

It can sense movement through thin walls, doors and glass, though not what is there. Lower the range, exclude zones or move it if it reacts to the next room.

### Does a presence sensor record video or sound?

No. Motion and radar sensors report whether, and sometimes where, someone is present. They do not capture images or sound.

### Will my cat or dog set it off?

Often, yes. Some radar sensors can ignore small targets or chosen zones, but none are perfect. In a home with pets, test it before relying on it for something like an alarm.
