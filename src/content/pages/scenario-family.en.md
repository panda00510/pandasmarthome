Many Singapore homes are shared by several generations: grandparents, parents, children, often a domestic helper, and visitors. A smart home that only one person knows how to use fails here first. This page brings together what our control, lock and lighting pages and our guides say about one situation: a household where everyone needs something different.

## Start with the people

Write down who lives in the home and what each person actually needs to do. The person who likes to tinker gets the full dashboard and the settings; everyone else gets a handful of obvious buttons; guests and a helper get access that needs no app and no account. See [controls for the whole household](/solutions/home-control/).

## Controls anyone can use

- **Wall switches keep working** exactly as before, whatever happens to the app, the hub or the internet.
- **A few scene buttons** by the door and the bed, labelled in the language people read.
- **A simple mode** for anyone who just wants lights, curtains and aircon, without the rest.

## Doors and access

- **A door code for each person,** rather than one shared code or a lent key, so one person's access can be removed without changing everyone else's.
- **Temporary codes** for visitors and contractors, removed afterwards.
- **A backup way in** in case of a flat battery or a fault. See [smart door locks](/guides/smart-door-lock-guide/) for the backup plans to check.
- **Decide what children can do:** whether they can open the main door with a code, or change the aircon.

Our guide to [access for a helper, children and guests](/guides/smart-home-helpers-kids-guests-access/) covers each of these in more detail.

## Looking out for older parents

Night lighting, door sensors and motion sensors reassure without any camera. Soft lights between the bed and the bathroom lower the chance of a trip in the dark, and a presence sensor can show normal activity in the morning. Agree on all of it with your parent first. A sensor cannot replace a visit, a call or proper care, and it can raise false alarms. See [a smart home for elderly parents](/guides/smart-home-for-elderly-parents/) and [camera-free fall detection with radar](/guides/radar-fall-detection/).

## Privacy and fairness

- **Tell everyone what is installed,** what it senses and whether anything is recorded.
- **Keep personal spaces private.** No camera or recording device in a helper's room or a bedroom, and no use of sensors or entry logs to track someone's time off.
- **Prefer local control** where you can, so routines and sensor data do not need to leave the home. See [smart home privacy: local or cloud](/guides/smart-home-privacy-local-vs-cloud/).

## Common questions

### Does everyone need the app?

No. Wall switches and keypads cover the everyday things, and a helper or guest never needs a personal account or an app on their own phone.

### Can we keep an eye on an older parent without cameras?

Yes. Night lighting, door and motion sensors and presence sensors give a lot of reassurance without any camera. Agree on what is installed with your parent first.

### Can I remove one person's door access?

Yes, on a digital lock with individual codes. Removing one code does not affect anyone else's.

### Should a helper be told what is installed?

Yes. Explain what is installed, what it senses and whether anything is recorded, before it goes live, and keep their own room free of cameras and recording devices.
