Most homes in Singapore are not new. They have wiring that was never planned for smart switches, walls that block Wi-Fi and aircon older than the apps that control it. None of that rules out a smart home. It just changes the order of the work: survey first, then choose devices that fit what is already there.

## What is different in an older home

- **Switch boxes.** Many have no neutral wire, which affects which smart switches can be used.
- **Wiring and distribution board.** Their condition decides whether anything should be dealt with before devices go in. That judgement is for a licensed electrical worker.
- **Wi-Fi.** Thick concrete and brick walls, metal doors and lift cores weaken the signal.
- **Aircon and doors.** Older units use infrared remotes, and older doors suit retrofit locks.

## How an upgrade usually goes

1. **A wiring and network survey** of what you have.
2. **A short plan** that says what to do first, what to leave and what needs more work.
3. **One area first,** often the living room or the lights and aircon you use daily.
4. **Extend** once the household has lived with it for a few weeks.

## What avoids opening walls

- Retrofit switch modules and no-neutral smart switches.
- Battery sensors for doors, windows, motion and leaks.
- Infrared controllers for split aircon.
- Clip-on curtain robots and retrofit door locks.
- Physical switches that keep working, so nobody is left behind.

## Common questions

### Does my older home need to be rewired?

Usually not. Retrofit options work with existing wiring. A licensed electrical worker can tell you whether your wiring needs attention first.

### Can I upgrade in stages?

Yes, and it is usually the best way. Start with one area, then extend as you learn what you use.

### Will it work with the devices I already own?

Very often, yes. Most popular brands integrate directly, and a few work only through a cloud account. We tell you which is which before you spend anything more.
