In a landed house, the first problem is rarely automation. It is coverage: a signal that reaches every floor, a gate and perimeter that need watching, and a household of several generations who each expect something different. We start with the foundations, then build the automations on top.

## What a larger home needs first

- **A structured network.** Wired runs to where access points will sit, so Wi-Fi does not depend on a single router at one end of the house. See our [Wi-Fi guide](/guides/wifi-for-smart-home/).
- **Gate and perimeter.** Gate status, door and window sensors and camera coverage on one dashboard.
- **Cameras that do more.** If you already have CCTV, an edge analytics box may add features without replacing it. See our guide on [adding AI to existing CCTV](/guides/ai-upgrade-existing-cctv/).
- **Controls for everyone.** Physical switches keep working, and wall keypads or a simple tablet help family members and helpers who never installed an app.

## How we approach it

1. **Walk the property,** floor by floor, noting walls, dead spots and where cables can run.
2. **Plan the network first,** then devices.
3. **Roll out in stages,** by floor or by function, so the house is usable at every step.

## Common questions

### Do I need a wired network for a landed house?

Wired runs to access points are more reliable than depending on Wi-Fi alone, especially across floors. We plan the cable routes with you, ideally before ceilings and walls are finished.

### Can cameras record locally?

Where the camera supports it, yes. Recording can stay on hardware in your home instead of a vendor cloud.

### Will the system still work if the internet is down?

The core runs locally. Automations, wall switches and sensors keep working. Remote access and cloud-only devices pause until the connection returns. Read more in our guide on [what keeps working offline](/guides/smart-home-without-internet/).
